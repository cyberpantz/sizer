## Your instinct is right, with one correction

Literal beats clever here. Someone arriving from a search for "will my bag fit ryanair" is anxious and skimming; a name that explains itself removes a step. And the design direction reinforces it — wayfinding systems name things plainly. Airport signs say BAGGAGE CLAIM, not "Reclaim."

The correction: **"literal" and "generic" aren't the same thing.** Fully generic names have three real costs.

- Effectively unregisterable as a trademark, so you can't stop anyone from launching a near-identical name
- The `.com` is almost certainly taken or expensive
- Exact-match domains **stopped conferring any SEO benefit years ago** — so the main argument people reach for isn't valid

What you want is **literal but ownable**: instantly comprehensible, still a name.

## Candidates

### Tier 1 — literal and ownable

**Sizer**
The airport object itself — the metal frame at the gate your bag has to fit inside. Literal to anyone who's flown, short, memorable, and it works as a verb. Fits the wayfinding direction exactly: it names a piece of airport infrastructure. Strongest candidate. `sizer.com` is likely taken; `thesizer.com`, `sizer.travel`, `sizer.app` are plausible.

**Gate Check**
Names the exact outcome the site helps you avoid, and everyone who's been through it knows the phrase. Slight ambiguity — gate-checking is also a normal service — but the association is right, and the anxiety it triggers is the anxiety that brought them.

**Bag Gauge**
"Gauge" is the measurement instrument *and* the airline term for the sizer frame. Precise, technical, on-theme with the design. Less immediately obvious than Sizer.

### Tier 2 — maximally literal

**Will It Fit**
Matches the actual search query almost word for word. Zero explanation needed. Reads a little like a utility than a brand, and it's a question rather than a noun — awkward in a sentence ("according to Will It Fit…").

**Carry-On Check** / **Bag Check**
Clear. Both likely crowded — "bag check" also means the coat-check counter, which muddies it.

**Luggage Finder** (your original)
Honest and clear, but describes the weaker half of the product. The differentiator isn't finding luggage — plenty of sites do that. It's the *verdict*. A name pointing at the recommendation rather than the ruling undersells what makes this defensible, and it's the most generic option on the list.

### Tier 3 — worth noting

**Cabin Index** (current prototype)
Fine name, wrong direction now. "Index" reads editorial and bookish, which suited the field-guide aesthetic you moved away from. Against the wayfinding system it's a mismatch.

---

## Recommendation

**Sizer**, with **Gate Check** as the alternate.

It's literal in the way that matters — it names the physical thing the product simulates — while still being a name you can own, trademark, and put on a departure board in condensed caps. It also survives scope growth: if the product later covers checked bags or personal items, "Sizer" still fits where "Carry-On Check" wouldn't.

If none of the domains work, **Will It Fit** is the safe fallback. It gives up brandability but loses nothing in comprehension.

---

## Domain strategy

1. Check `.com` first. It still matters for credibility in a trust-led product, more than in most categories.
2. `.travel`, `.app`, `.co` are acceptable fallbacks. **Avoid `.io`** — it reads developer-tool, wrong audience entirely.
3. Avoid hyphens and avoid deliberate misspellings.
4. Buy the obvious typo variants if cheap.
5. Grab matching social handles at the same time, even if unused at launch — they're free and they're gone later.
