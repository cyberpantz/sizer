# ULCC enforcement — cross-carrier evidence

Evidence about how ultra-low-cost carriers enforce baggage policy, kept
separately because it outlives any individual airline. The Spirit record was
deleted when the airline ceased operations; this did not stop being true.

## Per-catch commissions to gate staff

> "Frontier and Spirit paid **$26 million** to gate agents and other personnel
> between 2022 and 2023 to catch passengers allegedly not following airline bag
> policies, often forcing those passengers to **pay a bag fee or miss their
> flight**. Frontier personnel can earn **as much as $10 for each bag** a
> passenger is forced to check at the gate."
>
> — US Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, press release,
> 26 Nov 2024. **Read in full.** The 55-page report itself has not been read.

Bearing on the model:

1. **`staff_incentive` is real and documented**, not inferred from the business
   model. It is the only enforcement component in the dataset with a tier-1
   source behind it.
2. **It justifies the ULCC prior of 9** independently of any hand-assigned score
   — which matters, because the priors were otherwise fitted against numbers the
   same author wrote.
3. **It produced the `agentCommission` advisory**, the only one that fires on a
   CLEARS verdict, because a commission describes an outcome the bands cannot
   hold: a compliant bag charged anyway.

The $26M figure is **joint** across both carriers and must never be attributed
to one alone. Spirit's own per-bag amount was never verified — a $5 figure
appeared in a syndicated summary that was not opened.
