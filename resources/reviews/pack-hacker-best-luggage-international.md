## Their testing method — genuinely reproducible

> "We filled every bag with gear and took it on a set course that included a smooth surface, bricks or cobblestones, and grass. Once we rated how each bag handled each surface, we created a weighted average equation that prioritized smooth surfaces, noted how well each bag did on rough surfaces, and slightly considered how it could go on something like grass."

| Bag | Weighted score |
|---|---|
| Monos Carry-On | **7.05** |
| Travelpro Maxlite 5 21″ | 6.7 |
| Aer Carry-On Small | 6.7 |
| July Carry On | 6.4 |
| Osprey Transporter Wheeled 38 | *highest on brick, tied highest on grass* |

This is the only physical, numeric, method-stated test in any of the three roundups. It measures something real that we do not and should not measure. **Cite it; never restate it as ours.**

## Two wheels vs four — tested, not asserted

> "Four wheels are great on flat ground; however, if you're even slightly off roading (think cracked concrete, bricks, cobblestones, etc.), the second two wheels become pretty useless. Additionally, two-wheeled luggage typically has more prominent, durable wheels."

The Osprey Transporter — a **two-wheeler** — scored highest of every bag on brick and tied highest on grass.

**This corrects our own filter note.** We wrote that rollers win on value, build or capacity. The better answer, and the tested one, is that **rollers win on rough ground** — which is precisely the European city case our audience is flying into.

## Where they stop, in their own words

Repeated across the piece:

- Aer: *"we prefer the Small version, as it's more compatible with the most popular airlines"*
- Away Flex: *"The expansion system may affect carry on compliance"*
- Away Flex: *"ensure that the dimensions of the larger size work for the airplane you're flying"*
- Bellroy: *"if you regularly fly strict airlines where every kilo matters"*
- FAQ: **"Sometimes, bag manufacturers make bags that are meant to be used as a carry on but are slightly too large to be used with some airlines. We recommend checking your specific airline before flying."**

Testers since 2017, a century of combined experience, a physical rolling course — and on the size question they hand the reader off. That last quote is the clearest statement of our market any of the three sources produced, and it was written by a competitor for luggage attention.
