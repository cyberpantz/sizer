## Methodology / criteria used by the reviewer

- **Carry-on size baseline:** 22" L × 14" W × 9" H (stated as the common US airline limit). All picks meet this, so the article compares **liter capacity** and **empty weight** instead of dimensions.
- **Hard-sided vs. soft-sided:** most picks are hard-sided spinners; for bags, strap and padding type is called out instead.
- **Compartments:** pocket/organization layout, especially fast laptop and liquids access.
- **Warranty:** noted per item; most cover manufacturing defects, not wear and tear.

## Notable differentiators worth capturing as product attributes

- **Wheels:** 4-wheel spinners glide on smooth floors; 2-wheel recessed wheels are recommended for cobblestones/uneven surfaces. Aer has a one-button brake; Roverlund's skateboard-style wheels roll freely (no self-braking), while Hinomoto wheels (used by Away) are somewhat self-braking.
- **Shell material hierarchy cited:** aluminum (most durable, heaviest) > polycarbonate > ABS (cheapest, least durable).
- **Packing systems:** Solgaard's compression shelving unfolds into a hanging closet; Away/Quince both use internal compression + removable laundry bag; Travelpro VersaPack+ ships with packing cubes, two shoe bags, laundry bag, and a waterproof toiletries compartment; Cotopaxi has sewn-in mesh cubes with a butterfly opening.
- **Security/hardware:** Away and Quince both use YKK zippers and TSA-approved combination locks; aluminum cases use clamps instead of zips.
- **Expansion + weight indicator:** Béis expands ~3" and has a built-in overweight indicator (red mark) for airlines enforcing carry-on weight limits.
- **Laptop access:** Calpak Hue front pocket and Roverlund's external laptop compartment are called out for fast security screening.
- **Repairability:** Rimowa repair locations in most major cities; Travelpro favored by flight crews for longevity and repairability; Eagle Creek repair-and-replace program.
