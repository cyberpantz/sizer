## Testing methodology (T+L's own description)

1. **Pack test** — each carry-on is filled with a four-day load: 6 shirts, 3 pairs of pants, 5 pairs of socks, 5 pairs of underwear, 2 jackets, 2 pairs of shoes, 1 small + 1 large toiletry bag. They note how hard the zippers/compartments are to close and whether the expander was needed.
2. **Maneuverability** — pushed and pulled over carpet, concrete, and hard floors; run through an obstacle course for tight turns; simulated concrete curbs.
3. **Fit test** — placed in an overhead bin on a simulated airplane set with real plane seats.
4. **Durability** — struck several times with a metal baseball bat; thrown off a counter/table drop. Some testers also stood/jumped on the bag.
5. **Long-term** — editors travel with the bags for ~6 months and the article is updated with long-term findings.

## Buying criteria the article uses

- **Hardside vs. softside:** hardside protects fragile contents better and looks sleeker but scuffs/dents and shows wear; softside resists visible beating and flexes for overpackers but is less protective and weaker in bad weather.
- **Weight:** stressed heavily. Tested wheeled carry-ons ranged **3.99–12.9 lb**; the article advises against anything heavier than that range.
- **Organization:** baseline is one main compartment plus one or two mesh pockets; extras worth seeking are shoe separator, laptop sleeve, laundry bag, extra zip sections, exterior pockets, AirTag holder.

## FAQ facts worth reusing as reference data

- **Standard carry-on dimensions:** 22 × 14 × 9 in, but they vary by airline — which is why several oversize picks (23 × 15 × 10, 23 × 16 × 9) are on the list anyway.
- **Weight limits:** vary; JetBlue and American are cited as having no carry-on weight restriction, while some international carriers cap at 22 lb. Dimension limits are enforced more consistently than weight in the authors' experience.
- **Liquids:** TSA quart bag; no container over 3.4 oz. Solid toiletries (powder makeup, bar soap, solid deodorant) are unrestricted.
- **AirTags:** allowed in carry-ons; higher-power GPS trackers should be checked against the specific airline.
- **Format:** rolling suitcases are called the lowest-effort option; backpacks and duffels are preferred for cobblestones or hands-free travel.
